CFPB Fair Lending Report - AI/Model-Based Credit Decisions Guidance
Source: https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_fair-lending-report_fy-2023.pdf (June 2024)

CONSUMER FINANCIAL PROTECTION CIRCULAR 2023-03: Adverse Action Notification
Requirements and the Proper Use of Sample Forms (Applies to AI/Complex Models)

On September 19, 2023, the CFPB released guidance clarifying that under ECOA and
Regulation B, lenders must provide applicants a statement of SPECIFIC and ACCURATE
reasons when taking adverse action, and CANNOT simply use generic sample adverse action
forms or checklists if they do not reflect the actual reason for the denial. This requirement is
especially important given the growth of advanced algorithms and machine learning models in
credit underwriting.

KEY PRINCIPLE: The legal requirement to explain adverse action reasons exists to help
consumers understand why they were denied, improve their future creditworthiness, and detect
mistakes or illegal discrimination. A denial reason generated by an AI model must still be
specific enough to be meaningful to the applicant -- a generic "did not meet model criteria" is
not sufficient, regardless of how the underlying model works.

FAIR LENDING SUPERVISION FINDINGS ON CREDIT SCORING MODELS (2023):
The CFPB directed institutions using credit scoring / automated models to:
1. Document the specific business need each model serves.
2. Document specific standards for assessing whether a model serves that stated business need.
3. Test credit scoring models for prohibited-basis disparities (i.e., check whether the model's
   outputs differ systematically by race, sex, age, national origin, or other protected
   characteristics, even if the model doesn't explicitly use those characteristics as inputs).
4. Document considerations given to how disparities are assessed against the stated business
   need.
5. Develop a process for considering a range of "less discriminatory alternative" models --
   i.e., if a less-discriminatory model exists that serves the same business purpose comparably
   well, institutions are expected to consider adopting it.
6. Test and validate the methodology used to identify the "principal reasons" cited in adverse
   action notices -- meaning the reason codes given to a denied applicant must be genuinely
   validated to reflect what actually drove the model's decision, not just a plausible-sounding
   explanation.

JOINT STATEMENT ON AUTOMATED SYSTEMS (April 25, 2023):
The CFPB, DOJ, EEOC, and FTC jointly committed to enforcement against discrimination and
bias in automated systems, explicitly including those marketed as "AI." The statement makes
clear that using an algorithm or AI model does NOT exempt an institution from anti-
discrimination law -- "automated systems and advanced technology" is not an excuse for
lawbreaking behavior.

DIGITAL DISCRIMINATION AND DATA USE:
Creditors may violate ECOA/Regulation B if they use data (including from data brokers, or
proxies correlated with protected characteristics such as ZIP code) in ways that create
discriminatory targeting, steering, or disparate impact -- even if no prohibited characteristic is
used directly as a model input. A facially neutral factor (like ZIP code) can still create unlawful
disparate impact if it correlates strongly with a protected class and isn't justified by a legitimate,
non-discriminatory business need that can't be achieved through less discriminatory means.

MOST FREQUENTLY CITED VIOLATIONS IN 2023 EXAMINATIONS (for reference on real risk
areas):
- Discrimination in a credit transaction, or improperly requiring a spouse's signature.
- Improper evaluation of age or receipt of public assistance income.
- Adverse action notice failures: missing the 30-day notice window, or providing insufficient/
  non-specific reasons for the action taken.
- Failure to collect required monitoring information (race, ethnicity, sex) for certain
  application types.

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY FOR A DECISIONING SYSTEM:
Any automated or model-based credit decision must be paired with a specific, accurate,
validated reason for adverse action -- not a generic score or internal policy citation. High
disparate-impact risk factors (age, geography/ZIP code as proxy, public assistance income)
require particular scrutiny before being used as inputs, and if used, must be tested for
disparate impact against protected classes.
